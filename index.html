MuAPI Review (2026): Models, Costs and the Fine Print

MuAPI is an aggregator: one endpoint in front of models from OpenAI, Google, Kling, ByteDance, Black Forest Labs and MiniMax, sold on price and convenience.

Quick verdictAggregators are worth it when you genuinely switch models often, run comparisons, or want to ship a feature before deciding which provider wins. This one presents that case well, with a playground, rankings, a status page and a CLI. It is worth less if your product calls exactly one model in production forever, because you are then paying a middle layer to forward a request you could send yourself. If video generation is the whole job, a focused service such as AI Video API is a smaller surface to reason about. The caveat is that 'lowest cost' is the vendor's claim, not a measurement.

The pitch on the front page

One API for all AI models, described as the lowest cost API for image, video and audio generation. Two buttons, Start Building and Read Documentation, and a statistics strip underneath. The site is translated into twenty languages, which tells you something about who it is chasing. Around the core API sit a playground, model rankings, a workflow builder, agents, apps, a studio, integrations, an MCP and CLI page, billing and usage views, and a white label option. That last one is the quiet signal: a lot of aggregator revenue comes from people reselling generation inside their own product, and the feature list is arranged for exactly that customer rather than for a hobbyist trying one prompt.

MuAPI counts its own models two different ways

The hero strip says 500 or more AI models in one API. Two scrolls down, the providers section says access 250 or more optimized models with a single API. Both sentences are on the same page. There is a plausible innocent explanation, something like total catalogue versus the subset that has been tuned, but the page does not say which, and that is the kind of detail worth noticing before you build a model picker around a number. The lesson is small and general: on aggregator sites, counts are marketing units. What matters for your project is whether the four specific models you care about are present, current, and priced in a way you can predict, and no headline figure answers that.

Who is actually behind the endpoint

The providers listed are OpenAI, Google, Kling, ByteDance, Black Forest Labs and MiniMax, with a link to a fuller providers page. The promotional bar at the top of the site advertises a 20 percent discount on Seedance 2, a 28 percent discount on Seedance 2.5 and the arrival of MiniMax H3, so the catalogue is clearly being refreshed as upstream models land. The footer names Veo 3, Wan 2.2, Flux Kontext, Seedance, Midjourney V7, Kling and HiDream among its model pages. Two things follow. Availability tracks upstream releases, which is the point of using an aggregator. And any discount you see is a campaign, not a rate card, so read the pricing page rather than the banner.

The economics of paying a middle layer

An aggregator adds a margin and removes a pile of integration work, and whether that trade is good depends on how many providers you would otherwise maintain. Three or four, with different auth schemes and different async conventions, and the middle layer pays for itself in the first month. Exactly one, called a million times, and you are paying rent on a request you could send directly. The stat strip also gestures at no subscription and at how failed tasks are handled, but the accompanying figures are not spelled out on that page, so confirm both in the documentation before assuming anything about charges on errors. Run your own arithmetic on your own volume, not on a homepage claim.

When a narrower service fits better

Breadth has a cost that nobody advertises: a catalogue of hundreds of models means hundreds of parameter shapes, and your code ends up carrying a translation layer anyway. If the entire requirement is video generation, AI Video API is the other end of that spectrum, a plain REST API for video tasks with no interface wrapped around it and nothing to learn beyond submitting a job and collecting the result. Fewer options, fewer decisions, less surface to monitor. Breadth is the right answer while you are still choosing; focus is the right answer once you have chosen and you just want the queue to keep moving.

Four steps before you integrate

  1. Name your four models
    Write down the exact models your product needs, not the categories. Then search the catalogue for each one. A headline count is irrelevant if the fourth name on your list is missing.
  2. Run the playground first
    Generate the same prompt through the playground before writing a line of client code. You are checking output quality and turnaround, which no documentation page can tell you.
  3. Read the failure terms
    Find out in the documentation what happens to a failed or cancelled task and whether it consumes credit. This is the single line item people forget until a bad batch drains a balance.
  4. Compare against going direct
    Price your real monthly volume both through the aggregator and against the upstream provider you use most. If you only ever call one model, the middle layer has to earn its margin some other way.

Questions people ask

Is MuAPI really the cheapest option?

The site describes itself as the lowest cost API for image, video and audio generation, and compares itself against direct provider APIs. That is a vendor claim with no methodology published on the page. The only honest test is pricing your own workload, at your own volume, against the provider you would otherwise call directly.

How many models does it actually have?

The home page says 500 or more in one place and 250 or more in another, without reconciling the two. Rather than pick a number, check the catalogue for the specific models you intend to call and confirm the version, because that is what your integration depends on.

Which providers are included?

OpenAI, Google, Kling, ByteDance, Black Forest Labs and MiniMax are named on the front page, with a providers hub for the full list. The footer also links model pages for Veo 3, Wan 2.2, Flux Kontext, Seedance, Midjourney V7, Kling and HiDream.

Do I pay for failed generations?

The statistics strip mentions failed tasks but the page does not spell out the terms in readable form, so this is one to confirm in the documentation before you run a large batch. Any generation service can fail on a bad prompt, and how that is billed matters more than the headline rate.

Is there a subscription?

The home page suggests there is not one, describing the service in terms of usage rather than monthly tiers, and it links a separate pricing page. Read that page for the current structure, since credit systems and minimum top-ups change more often than the marketing copy around them.

What if I only need video?

Then most of the catalogue is weight you carry for nothing. AI Video API covers video generation tasks through a plain REST interface with no extra surface, which is easier to monitor and easier to reason about when something in the queue goes wrong at three in the morning.
